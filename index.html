About

I came to AI safety sideways. My degree is in energy engineering and my thesis is about stabilising India's carbon market. In 2025 I got stuck on a simple question: if a model tells you what you want to hear, how would you know?

So I built a probe for it. Then a benchmark for whether models admit using hints. It didn't stop. Most of my projects start with something people repeat without checking, like "models explain their reasoning" or "we test AI before release", and try to measure whether it holds. Often it doesn't, and I'd rather publish the number than the story. The limitations sections are the part I'm proudest of.

I'm Indian, and most safety tooling isn't built for here. So the newest work is specifically about India: voice-clone scam calls in Hindi and Tamil, medicine brand names that chatbots get dangerously wrong, and the criminal code that was replaced in 2024 while models kept citing the old one.
